My journey into the complexities of biomedical science began during my undergraduate career, where I had the privilege of joining Dr. Karine Le Roch’s laboratory at UC Riverside. Driven by a deep curiosity about infectious diseases and a desire to contribute to global health solutions, I immersed myself in cutting-edge genomic and transcriptomic research. Dr. Le Roch’s lab, renowned for its pioneering work on the causative agent of malaria, Plasmodium falciparum, provided the perfect environment for me to transition from textbook concepts to hands-on, impactful scientific discovery. From my first day in the lab, I was challenged to think critically and operate with a high degree of precision. My research primarily focused on understanding the complex life cycle and regulatory mechanisms of the malaria parasite. Given that Plasmodium falciparum relies on tightly orchestrated gene expression to evade the host immune system, our team sought to uncover the epigenetic and structural factors governing its genome. Under the mentorship of Dr. Le Roch, I gained proficiency in advanced molecular biology techniques, including next-generation sequencing (NGS) library preparation and CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing, while also mastering bioinformatic pipelines to interpret complex RNA-seq and ChIP-seq datasets. Bridging the gap between raw biological samples and data-driven insights transformed how I approach problem-solving in modern biology. Complementing my rigorous academic research, my dedication to public health and clinical preparation found a home in UCR’s Medical Emergency Education Project (MEEP). Recognizing that health disparities extend from cellular mechanisms to community education, I initially joined MEEP’s leadership team as the Outreach Officer. In this role, I spearheaded initiative-driven public health campaigns, coordinating hands-on CPR, AED, and emergency preparedness workshops for underserved communities within the Inland Empire. Empowering local youth and bystanders with life-saving skills gave real-world purpose to my scientific education. My dedication to MEEP's mission and my proven track record of collaborative leadership recently culminated in my election as the organization's President. In this executive executive role, I oversee the strategic direction of our three pillars—Outreach, Education, and Community. I guide our board of directors to expand our educational reach, secure local partnerships, and ensure the operational growth of our student-led emergency medicine initiative. Ultimately, my experiences in the Le Roch lab and at the helm of UCR MEEP have shaped my identity as both a scientist and a leader. They have instilled in me a profound appreciation for functional genomics, a gift for cross-functional management, and a lasting commitment to biomedical research and healthcare equity. As I move forward in my professional career, this dual foundation of rigorous scientific training and community-focused leadership continues to serve as the bedrock of my pursuits.
